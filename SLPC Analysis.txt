I. Introduction:
The paper presents research on the polarity classification of the verses of The Holy Quran. The main goal of this analysis is the detection of the regularity and occurrence of negative (polarized) statements for any group as (falsely) claimed over the internet (trend). Including only 6 English-translation of the book.[1]
        The algorithm for sentence-level polarity detection used is TextBlob. [2] The data from the algorithm is divided into different intensity levels. However, when using sentences as the basic units to which analysis is applied, it is always important to look at their semantic and morphological analysis. As expected the whole sentence is useful for the identification of sentence polarity rather than only words and their occurrence. Although this paper includes only sentence-level polarity classification, the inter-verses relations such as discourse relations are small factors in the analysis, they aren’t ignored or left out but accepted as the original or (new) order and facts of the Holy Quran. 


II. Sentence-Level Polarity Classification  (SLPC)
Sentence-level polarity detection is to decide whether a given sentence expresses either an overall positive or negative opinion. Thus, all sentences to be classified are assumed to be subjective and carry either positive [+1], negative [-1], or neutral [0] overall polarity. Moreover, according to Wiegand et al. [2010] at the document level, text classification relies very much on redundancy and there are so many cues suggesting positive polarity is more likely than negative polarity. [3]
        
 
III. English Translation of The Holy Quran
All translation data is exported from Islam-awakened  [4] and the translators are selected from personal preference and are well-known in South Asia. Removing and minimizing the reader’s personal preference issues due to different sects or schools of Islam. [5] [6]
 
IV. Algorithm for SLPC
The algorithm is NOT trained or made on or with verses of The Holy Quran and it is the secondary objective of this paper to enlighten the un-bias analysis. The initial training data can be found online [7] The dedicated algorithm that is trained on verses of the Holy Quran will be biased from a Muslim’s point of view. That study will be addressed in the future. 
        Sentence-level objectivity classification was included in this paper but removed after some consideration, keeping inter-verse relations and religious opinion values as the main causes. 


V. Sentence-Level Subjectivity 
Subjectivity classification classifies sentences into two classes: subjective and objective (Wiebe et al., 1999). An objective sentence expresses some factual information, while a subjective sentence usually gives personal views and opinions. From Muslims’ point of view, even the opinion mentioned in Holy Quran is a proper fact.
        All sentence-level subjectivity classification data isn’t included in this paper but may be added in the future as a separate study.

This paper included the subjectivity classification before, Following are the examples and results of sentence-level subjectivity classification analysis. Where [0.0] is very objective and [1.0] is very subjective.

But while checking these verses it is obvious that even subjective-classified verse is a fact and objective for Muslim readers, The algorithm classified these verses according to its training model made for English literature but not religious values and beliefs. The below analysis is totally based on the perspective separate from religious belief biases. 
         The question here is how subjective and objective verses are classified, take a look at the above verses the objective one includes the phrase “But if you do not do this, and you can never do this” that is a fact as a sentence to some extents, and the subjective one includes “Submit to your Lord Who created you and those who were before you” it is considered as a "Fact” in Islam because it is mentioned in The Holy Quran, but we have the opposing opinions from people of different religion and belief.

* Negative  -1 
* Neutral     0 
* Positive   +1
