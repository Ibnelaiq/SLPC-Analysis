I. Introduction:
The paper presents research on the polarity classification of the verses of The Holy Quran. The main goal of this analysis is to detect the regularity and occurrence of negative (polarized) statements as they are falsely claimed over the internet (trend). This research includes only 6 English translations of the book. [1]
The algorithm used for sentence-level polarity detection is TextBlob. [2] The data from the algorithm is divided into different intensity levels. However, when using sentences as the basic units for analysis, it is important to also consider their semantic and morphological analysis. As expected, the whole sentence is useful for identifying sentence polarity, rather than just individual words and their occurrence. Although this paper includes only sentence-level polarity classification, inter-verse relations such as discourse relations are small factors in the analysis. They are not ignored but accepted as the original or new order and facts of The Holy Quran.

II. Sentence-Level Polarity Classification  (SLPC)
Sentence-level polarity detection is to decide whether a given sentence expresses either an overall positive or negative opinion. Thus, all sentences to be classified are assumed to be subjective and carry either positive [+1], negative [-1], or neutral [0] overall polarity. Moreover, according to Wiegand et al. [2010] at the document level, text classification relies very much on redundancy and there are so many cues suggesting positive polarity is more likely than negative polarity. [3]
        
 
III. English Translation of The Holy Quran
All translation data is exported from Islam-awakened  [4] and the translators are selected from personal preference and are well-known in South Asia. Removing and minimizing the reader’s preference issues due to different sects or schools of Islam. [5] [6]
 
IV. Algorithm for SLPC
The algorithm is NOT trained or made on or with verses of The Holy Quran and it is the secondary objective of this paper to enlighten the un-bias analysis. The initial training data can be found online [7] The dedicated algorithm that is trained on verses of the Holy Quran will be biased from a Muslim’s point of view. That study will be addressed in the future. 
        Sentence-level objectivity classification was included in this paper but removed after some consideration, keeping inter-verse relations and religious opinion values as the main causes. 


V. Sentence-Level Subjectivity 
Subjectivity classification classifies sentences into two classes: subjective and objective (Wiebe et al., 1999). An objective sentence expresses some factual information, while a subjective sentence usually gives personal views and opinions. From Muslims’ point of view, even the opinion mentioned in Holy Quran is a proper fact.
        All sentence-level subjectivity classification data isn’t included in this paper but may be added in the future as a separate study. 






VI. Sentence-Level Subjectivity (SLS) Data
Following are the examples and results of sentence-level subjectivity classification analysis. Where [0.0] is very objective and [1.0] is very subjective.


* Strongly Subjective


“O Mankind, submit to your Lord Who created you and those who were before you; in this way, only      you may expect to save yourselves”
 (2:21)


* Strongly Objective


“But if you do not do this, and you can never do this, then fear the Fire which has been prepared for the disbelievers and which shall have men and stones for fuel”
 (2:24)


* Neutral (Neither proper subjective nor objective) 


“It is He Who has made the earth a bed for you and the sky a canopy; and it is He Who sends down rain from above for the growth of every kind of food products for your sustenance. So, when you know this, you should not set up equals to rank with Allah”
 (2:22)


It is obvious that even verses classified as subjective are considered facts and objective by Muslim readers. The algorithm classifies these verses based on its training model, which was created using English literature, not religious values and beliefs. The analysis below is based on a perspective that is separate from religious beliefs and biases. 
         In the above verses, the very-objectively-classified verse includes the phrase "But if you do not do this, and you can never do this" which is considered a fact to some extent. The very-subjectively-classified verse includes "Submit to your Lord Who created you and those who were before you". This sentence includes opinions and It is considered a fact (objective) in Islam. However, there are opposing opinions on this from people of different religions and beliefs.

The polarity of each verse lies between the range.
* Negative  -1 
* Neutral     0 
* Positive   +1
