I. Introduction:
The paper presents research and results on the polarity classification of the verses of the holy Quran. The main goal of the analysis is the detection of the regularity of negative-polarised statements as falsely claimed over the internet (trend). Including an analysis of only 6 English-translation of the book, and its authors. [1]
        The algorithm for sentence-level polarity detection used is TextBlob. [2] The data from the algorithm is divided into different intensity levels. However, when using sentences as the basic units  to which analysis is applied, it is always important to look at their semantic and morphological analysis. As expected the whole sentence is useful for the identification of sentence polarity rather than only words and their occurrence. Although this paper includes only sentence-level polarity classification, the inter-verses relations such as discourse relations are small factors in the sentence-level polarity classification analysis of (the Holy Quran), they aren’t ignored or left out but accepted as the original or chronological order of the Holy Quran. 


II. Sentence-Level Polarity Classification  (SLPC)
sentence-level polarity detection is to decide whether a given sentence expresses either an overall positive or negative opinion. Thus, all sentences to be classified are assumed to be subjective and carry either positive [+1], negative [-1], or neutral [0] overall polarity. Moreover, according to Wiegand et al. [2010] at the document level, text classification relies very much on redundancy and there are so many cues suggesting positive polarity is more likely than negative polarity. [3]
        
 
III. English Translation of The Holy Quran
All translation data is exported from Islam-awakened [4] and the translators are selected from personal preference and are well-known in South Asia. Removing and minimizing the reader’s personal preference issues related to different sects or schools of Islam. [5] [6]
 
IV. Algorithm for SLPC
The algorithm is NOT trained or made on or with verses of The Holy Quran and it is the secondary objective of this paper to enlighten the un-bias analysis. The initial training data can be found online [7] The dedicated algorithm that is trained on verses of the Holy Quran will be biased from a Muslim’s point of view. That study will be addressed in the future. 
        Sentence-level objectivity classification was included in this paper but removed after some consideration, keeping inter-verse relations and opinion values as the main objections. 


V. Sentence-Level Subjectivity 
Subjectivity classification classifies sentences into two classes: subjective and objective (Wiebe et al., 1999). An objective sentence expresses some factual information, while a subjective sentence usually gives personal views and opinions. From Muslims’ point of view, even the opinion mentioned in Holy Quran is a proper fact.
        All sentence-level subjectivity classification data isn’t included in this paper but may be added in the future as a separate study. 




VI. Polarity Classification


The polarity of each verse lies between the range.
* Negative  -1 
* Neutral     0 
* Positive   +1


The values from the algorithm are categorized into several categories for better analysis and visual representation.
